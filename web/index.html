    If you get a call like this

    No police officer, CBI, customs or court arrests anyone over a phone or video call, and no agency asks you to move money to "verify" it.

    During the call

    1. Hang up. You do not need permission to end the call.
    2. Tell someone you trust. Scammers rely on you staying alone and scared.
    3. Report the number on Chakshu at sancharsaathi.gov.in, even if you paid nothing.

    If you already sent money

    1. Call 1930 straight away. Reporting within the first hour gives the best chance of the money being frozen.
    2. Call your bank and ask them to block the transaction and your cards.
    3. Write down the transaction IDs and keep screenshots of the call log and messages.
    4. File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in with those details.

    How well it works

    How it works

    Every line of the call goes through three steps, all inside your browser. Nothing you hear is sent to an AI server.

    1. Read each line

      Pattern rules written for Hindi, Hinglish and English spot phrases like "arrest warrant" or "safe account". A trained classifier reads character patterns, so misspellings and speech-recognition slips still count.

    2. Follow the script

      Satark keeps track of which scam stages the call has reached and in what order. Authority and fear followed by secrecy or money is the digital-arrest pattern.

    3. Warn early

      As soon as two stages line up, Satark tells you to hang up, usually before the money is mentioned. Genuine callers who say "we never ask for your OTP" lower the risk.

    Satark is a prototype built for INNOVEX 2026, problem statement PS05, AI-powered cybersecurity. Sample calls are written for this demo; names and numbers in them are made up. In microphone mode your browser's speech recognition turns audio into text, which in Chrome may use Google's servers. The scam detection itself always runs on your device.
